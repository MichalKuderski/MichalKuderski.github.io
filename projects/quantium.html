The business question

Is the new layout worth taking further?

The simulation asks a category manager to evaluate trial stores 77, 86, and 88 during February–April 2019. A sales increase alone is insufficient: the comparison store, the pre-trial pattern, and the customer count all affect the interpretation.

My original R Markdown builds monthly measures, matches stores, scales the comparison, and examines the trial. This case study adds a documented reconstruction from the repository’s raw transaction CSV, with corrections to the original code.

Finding
All three trial stores show positive sales differences versus their scaled controls. Store 77 has the largest relative difference; store 88 has the weakest match score.
Interpretation
Customer activity explains much of the sales difference at stores 77 and 86. At store 88, sales rise more than customer counts, suggesting a different spending pattern.
Recommendation
Prioritize a confirmatory test at stores similar to 77 and 86. Recheck store 88 against alternative controls before a broader rollout.

Method & analytical judgment

Make the comparison inspectable.

1. Preserve the source grain

Start with 264,836 product line items. Remove one exact duplicate and two 200-unit purchases from the same outlier customer. Retain 264,833 rows, 72,636 unique customers, and all product names. Excel serial dates resolve to July 2018–June 2019.

2. Build monthly measures

Aggregate sales and units; count distinct customer IDs and transaction IDs within each store-month. Keep line items, transactions, monthly customers, and people distinct. The matched analysis uses stores with 12 observed months.

3. Match using the pre-trial period

Exclude all three trial stores from the candidate pool. For both sales and customers, combine Pearson correlation with normalized magnitude similarity over July–January. Average the four components equally, rank candidates, and select the highest score; break ties by store ID.

4. Scale, compare, then qualify

Fit separate sales and customer scale factors on the seven pre-trial months. Apply them to controls in February–April. Preserve the sign of the difference and report the aggregate gap, followed by a product-scope sensitivity check.

Scale = Σ trial pre-period / Σ control pre-period
Expected trial-period value = scale × Σ control trial-period
Gap % = 100 × (Σ trial observed / expected − 1)

Monthly aggregates were independently checked against a separate CSV parser using integer-cent sales arithmetic. Array calculations rechecked all displayed scale factors and trial totals. The original project uses R; the published reconstruction ran in Python as an independent verification. A revised R companion is included and has not been executed in this environment.

What changed from the original analysis?
  • The original code selects the second-ranked candidate after excluding the trial store. The reconstruction selects the highest-ranked eligible non-trial control and excludes every trial store.
  • Assignments such as measureOverTimeSales <- measureOverTime followed by := can mutate data.table objects by reference. The revised method keeps monthly measures intact.
  • Original sales bands use unscaled control totals while the discrepancy calculation uses scaled totals; the customer-band branch also mutates shared objects. The published snapshot uses matched, scaled values consistently.
  • The original uses absolute percentage differences, a hard-coded degrees-of-freedom comment inconsistent with the seven pre-trial months, and a conclusion table that reuses the final store’s score table. Those outputs do not validate the significance statements in its conclusion.
  • The original input QVI_data.csv is absent. This reconstruction uses the available raw transaction CSV with explicit cleaning and product scope; it is not claimed to reproduce the original run exactly.

Limits for a rollout decision: Three non-randomized trials, a short comparison window, and matched controls leave confounding and sampling uncertainty unresolved. Balanced-panel eligibility depends on full-year coverage. Use a confirmatory design and check alternative controls, promotions, costs, and margin before committing to rollout.
