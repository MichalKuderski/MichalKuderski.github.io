Which services drive payment,
and what deserves a closer look?

I organized 2,460 published provider–procedure–place-of-service records into three related tables, wrote analytical SQL, and built a seven-sheet Excel workbook.

The aim was to explain spending concentration and provider variation with consistent metrics, then identify questions that the source alone cannot answer.

Finding
CPT 99214 accounts for $5.65M, or 20.8% of payment. The top five codes account for 55.6% of the cohort’s Medicare payment.
Interpretation
Submitted charges, allowed amounts, and Medicare payments answer different questions. Payment per service unit is a more defensible primary metric here than payment per summed beneficiary count.
Next decision
Examine high-volume codes and procedure mix first. Treat provider and city differences as leads for investigation before making staffing or access recommendations.

What the denominator changes: CMS beneficiary counts are distinct within each published row. Summing them can count the same person more than once. I use payment per service unit in the headline KPIs and keep beneficiary-based outputs labeled as approximate. Higher payment per unit also does not establish lower efficiency or worse care.
Explore the method & limitations

Model the source grain

One row per provider NPI × HCPCS code × place of service. Providers and procedure codes are reference tables; utilization contains volumes and payment measures.

providers (NPI)utilization (fact)hcpcs_codes (code)

Weight the metrics correctly

Estimated payment = sum of average payment × service units. Payment per unit = total payment ÷ total units. This differs from an unweighted average of provider averages.

Screen variation, then investigate

CTEs and window functions compare providers within specialty. Distribution summaries are descriptive screens; procedure mix, volume, and case mix need further analysis before labeling a provider inefficient.

Separate patterns from causes

The file cannot establish access barriers, clinical outcomes, appointment availability, or why telephone use differs. Provider geography is not patient geography. Follow-up needs new data and a defined comparison.

Provider outlier screeningCTEs · weighted mean · RANK() window function
PostgreSQL · 77 lines
WITH provider_metrics AS (
    -- Individual provider-level payment per service
    SELECT
        p.npi,
        p.first_name,
        p.last_org_name,
        p.credentials,
        p.city,
        p.provider_type                                                 AS specialty,
        SUM(u.tot_srvcs)                                                AS total_services,
        SUM(u.tot_benes)                                                AS sum_benes_approx,
        ROUND(SUM(u.avg_medicare_payment * u.tot_srvcs), 2)             AS total_payment,

        -- Provider's own payment per service
        ROUND(
            SUM(u.avg_medicare_payment * u.tot_srvcs) /
            NULLIF(SUM(u.tot_srvcs), 0),
        2)                                                              AS provider_pay_per_svc,

        -- Specialty average payment per service (window function over specialty group)
        ROUND(
            SUM(SUM(u.avg_medicare_payment * u.tot_srvcs)) OVER (PARTITION BY p.provider_type) /
            NULLIF(SUM(SUM(u.tot_srvcs)) OVER (PARTITION BY p.provider_type), 0),
        2)                                                              AS specialty_avg_pay_per_svc

    FROM utilization u
    JOIN providers p ON u.npi = p.npi
    GROUP BY p.npi, p.first_name, p.last_org_name, p.credentials, p.city, p.provider_type
),

-- Add deviation from specialty mean and rank within specialty
ranked_providers AS (
    SELECT
        *,
        -- Absolute dollar deviation from specialty average
        ROUND(provider_pay_per_svc - specialty_avg_pay_per_svc, 2)     AS deviation_from_avg,

        -- Percent deviation from specialty average
        ROUND(
            (provider_pay_per_svc - specialty_avg_pay_per_svc) /
            NULLIF(specialty_avg_pay_per_svc, 0) * 100,
        1)                                                              AS pct_deviation,

        -- Rank within specialty (highest payment per service = rank 1)
        RANK() OVER (
            PARTITION BY specialty
            ORDER BY provider_pay_per_svc DESC
        )                                                               AS specialty_rank
    FROM provider_metrics
)

-- Final output: filter to providers who deviate > 50% from specialty mean
-- and have at least 10 services (low-volume providers can appear as outliers by chance)
SELECT
    specialty,
    specialty_rank,
    npi,
    CONCAT(first_name, ' ', last_org_name)  AS provider_name,
    credentials,
    city,
    total_services,
    sum_benes_approx,
    total_payment,
    provider_pay_per_svc,
    specialty_avg_pay_per_svc,
    deviation_from_avg,
    pct_deviation,
    CASE
        WHEN pct_deviation > 50  THEN 'HIGH OUTLIER'
        WHEN pct_deviation < -50 THEN 'LOW OUTLIER'
        ELSE 'Within Range'
    END                                     AS outlier_flag

FROM ranked_providers
WHERE ABS(pct_deviation) > 50
  AND total_services >= 10
ORDER BY specialty, pct_deviation DESC;

A ±50% threshold and a minimum of 10 service units are screening choices, not a formal significance test. A flagged provider needs further review of volume and procedure mix.

Telephone-coded share by cityConditional aggregation · explicit denominator · provider counts
PostgreSQL · 28 lines
WITH city_services AS (
    SELECT
        INITCAP(TRIM(p.city)) AS city,
        COUNT(DISTINCT p.npi) AS total_provider_count,
        COUNT(DISTINCT p.npi) FILTER (
            WHERE u.hcpcs_cd IN ('99441', '99442', '99443')
        ) AS telephone_provider_count,
        SUM(u.tot_srvcs) AS total_service_units,
        COALESCE(SUM(u.tot_srvcs) FILTER (
            WHERE u.hcpcs_cd IN ('99441', '99442', '99443')
        ), 0) AS telephone_service_units,
        COALESCE(SUM(u.avg_medicare_payment * u.tot_srvcs) FILTER (
            WHERE u.hcpcs_cd IN ('99441', '99442', '99443')
        ), 0) AS telephone_medicare_payment
    FROM utilization u
    JOIN providers p ON p.npi = u.npi
    WHERE p.state = 'IL' AND p.provider_type = 'Psychiatry'
    GROUP BY INITCAP(TRIM(p.city))
)
SELECT
    city, total_provider_count, telephone_provider_count,
    total_service_units, telephone_service_units,
    ROUND(telephone_medicare_payment, 2) AS telephone_medicare_payment,
    ROUND(100.0 * telephone_service_units
        / NULLIF(total_service_units, 0), 1) AS telephone_service_pct
FROM city_services
WHERE telephone_service_units > 0
ORDER BY telephone_service_units DESC, city;

Numerator: 99441–99443 service units. Denominator: all published service units in that provider city. Includes both total providers and telephone-code providers.

Check the source grainDuplicates · service volume · missing or negative payment
PostgreSQL · 13 lines
SELECT npi, hcpcs_cd, place_of_service, COUNT(*) AS row_count
FROM utilization
GROUP BY npi, hcpcs_cd, place_of_service
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;

-- Null, non-positive volume, and negative-payment checks.
SELECT
    COUNT(*) AS published_rows,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE tot_srvcs IS NULL OR tot_srvcs <= 0)
        AS invalid_service_rows,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE avg_medicare_payment IS NULL
        OR avg_medicare_payment < 0) AS invalid_payment_rows
FROM utilization;

These checks look for data problems before interpreting results. They do not certify the full dataset or establish clinical validity.
